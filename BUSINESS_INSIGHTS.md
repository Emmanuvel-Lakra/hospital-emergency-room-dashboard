# Hospital Emergency Room Dashboard --- Business Insights

## Project overview

This report summarizes the available KPI and PivotTable results in the
Hospital Emergency Room Excel dashboard workbook. It demonstrates how
dashboard figures can be translated into concise, evidence-based
operational observations.

## Five evidence-based findings

### 1. The dashboard summarizes 513 patient records

**Finding:** The patient-count summary reports 513 patients.

**Business meaning:** This gives management a headline measure of the
volume represented by the selected reporting period.

**Question for management:** How does this patient volume compare with
other months or reporting periods?

**Limitation:** The available summary shows January daily counts;
confirm the selected year and filter period before making longer-term
comparisons.

### 2. Average waiting time is approximately 36.32 minutes

**Finding:** The average waiting-time KPI is 36.3236 minutes, displayed
here as 36.32 minutes.

**Business meaning:** This provides a single summary measure of waiting
time across the records included in the calculation.

**Question for management:** Which dates, hours, or patient groups have
the longest waiting times?

**Limitation:** An overall average does not show the distribution of
waiting times or explain why waits occur.

### 3. The recorded average satisfaction score is 4.96

**Finding:** The dashboard summary reports an average patient
satisfaction score of approximately 4.96.

**Business meaning:** This is the average score calculated from the
records included in the dashboard.

**Question for management:** What rating scale was used, and how are
responses distributed across that scale?

**Limitation:** The scale and interpretation should be confirmed from
the source data or survey definition before describing this score as out
of 5 or labelling it high.

### 4. Admissions represent approximately 52.44% of the recorded patients

**Finding:** 269 patients are marked as admitted and 244 as not
admitted, out of 513 patients. These correspond to approximately 52.44%
and 47.56%, respectively.

**Business meaning:** In the selected records, the admitted group is
larger than the non-admitted group by 25 patients.

**Question for management:** How do admission proportions vary by date,
triage category, or other relevant clinical and operational factors?

**Limitation:** The workbook summary alone does not establish why a
patient was admitted or whether the admission proportion is appropriate.

### 5. Daily patient counts varied across January

**Finding:** The daily patient-count summary ranges from 10 patients on
19 January to 25 patients on 14 January.

**Business meaning:** The number of recorded patients was not the same
every day, which may be useful when reviewing staffing and operational
planning.

**Question for management:** Were staffing levels, shift patterns, or
other operational conditions different on higher-volume days?

**Limitation:** These counts show variation, not its cause. Further data
is needed before making staffing or demand explanations.

## Confirmed facts vs. questions to investigate

### Confirmed from the available workbook summaries

-   Total patient count: 513.
-   Average waiting time: approximately 36.32 minutes.
-   Average satisfaction score: approximately 4.96; scale not confirmed
    here.
-   Admitted: 269 patients (52.44%).
-   Not admitted: 244 patients (47.56%).
-   Highest daily count in the January summary: 25 on 14 January.
-   Lowest daily count in the January summary: 10 on 19 January.

### Not established by these figures alone

-   Why waiting times vary.
-   Why patients are admitted or not admitted.
-   Whether satisfaction is high or low without confirming the rating
    scale and benchmark.
-   Whether higher-volume days caused crowding or resource shortages.
-   Whether these results represent an entire year or only the currently
    selected period.

## Recommended follow-up analysis

1.  Confirm the reporting year and exact date range selected in the
    dashboard.
2.  Confirm the patient satisfaction rating scale and how missing
    responses are handled.
3.  Compare average and median waiting times, and review the longest
    waits.
4.  Compare daily patient volume with waiting time to see whether a
    relationship exists.
5.  Review admission status by relevant categories, where the data is
    available and appropriate.
6.  Compare results across months only after verifying that the filter
    selections and data coverage are consistent.
